# Relevant graph structures

What are the most relevant graph structures?


## Relevant structures of a graph

Specifically relevant graph structures (relevant to each graph being analyzed individually) which each graph would optimally implement in some structure include:

1. an abstraction spectrum, which is often useful across different graphs because it implements a 'type variant structure' (like 'identifying specific variants of a type') or an 'implementation structure' (like 'implementing a general intent with specific graphs') or a 'definition structure' (like 'specifying a general definition')

2. a 'connection to/integration of' errors/solutions, like an error/solution layer or integrating an error/solution graph, since applying problems/solutions to a graph increases the relevance/meaning of the graph, just like applying the definition of relevance increases the meaning

3. a relevance layer or relevant graph layer to connect the graph to meaning/relevance, like by identifying useful integrations of the graph into relevant graphs or identifying relevant queries of relevant graphs that generate/optimize the graph

4. an intent layer to indicate implementation structures of intents as 'queries of the graph' or 'queries of one of the graphs in the graph intent sequence (generate/organize/optimize/integrate graph)'

5. connections between graph layers to allow cross-layer queries

6. an iteration of interface analysis and of interface graphs (like 'limits/errors of a graph' and 'limit/error graphs of a graph') of a graph to identify the interface structures of a graph and identify the relevance of the graph

7. optimal points/queries/structures of a graph are useful to identify, so that these optimal points can be more trivially identified as connected to generative/descriptive variables of the graph, so that optimization structures of a graph can be predicted from early graph intents like 'generate/describe a graph'

8. Other graph variables like 'axes/layers/limits/errors/spectrums/definitions of distance/connection types/generative variables/descriptive variables/required input similarities/emergent output similarities/component graphs/integrated graphs/implementing graphs/relevant graphs/interface graphs/optimal points/optimal queries/implemented intents/priorities', such as how its useful to identify 'spectrums of increasing interface variables' (and 'correlated increasing spectrums') as 'axes of a graph', to identify 'emergent/required similarities in the graph' and 'optimal points of the graph' (as in 'as volatility increases, the graph applies these structures which indicate increasing volatility' and 'at this optimal point of volatility, the graph implements this structure', and 'as volatility increases, the graph applies a decrease in an intersecting organization spectrum of organization structures', such as a 'graph with distributed spectrums of volatility aligning with spectrums of organization in opposite directions', or a graph as a decreasing line indicating organization decreases as volatility increases with implementing structures at each point of the line)

9. Optimal variants of a graph can be useful to identify a 'graph of optimal variants of a graph' with intent to generate an optimal graph of graphs or a relevant graph by increasing the dimension of the graph, similar to how identifying optimization structures of a graph in general can be useful to identify a more optimal graph, where 'intersecting paths of optimizations of graphs' can identify optimal graphs and their variables

These structures are relevant to the graph itself, prioritizing one graph at a time which prioritizes one-graph intents like 'optimize a graph'.

Conditionally relevant graph structures (relevant to some graphs in some conditions) include: embedded spectrums, mixed connection types, mixed distance definitions, etc.


## Generally relevant graph structures

Generally relevant graph structures (relevant to multiple graphs like 'graph connections/sequences', or 'graphs of graphs') include:

1. connections between relevant graphs (graph sequences/networks like generative graph sequences or optimization graph sequences) to generate graphs of graphs and optimal queries of graph sequences/networks/graphs

2. connections between 'graph variables/abstractions/optimizations', which apply across graphs

3. integrations of relevant graphs generate new relevant graphs, using relevant graphs as inputs/components

4. structures of graphs like 'abstractions of a graph' which are useful for graph intents like 'identifying variables of an abstraction of a graph to generate variants of a graph'

These structures are relevant across graphs, prioritizing multi-graph intents like 'identify optimal graph sequences/networks'.

Optimizing one graph can identify/derive relevant structures like 'optimizations' across graphs, but optimizing graphs of graphs is likelier to generate insights applicable across graphs.

The difference between these is starting with one graph with the intent of optimizing it so it can be integrated with graph connections/sequences/graphs, and starting with a graph of graphs that is already relevant and identifying the limits of that relevance through specification/organization/optimization/implementation.
