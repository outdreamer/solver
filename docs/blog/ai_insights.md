# AI Insights

AI insights are useful to identify and apply.

For example, insights like the following are useful to identify:
- AI is a 'learning' function, which can be replaced by a derivation/inference/optimization function
- AI can be optimally applied to learn the general intent of 'optimization' (such as by comparing different optimization structures to identify more optimal or equivalent optimization structures, or implementing the definition of relevance/optimally)
- AI is a 'prediction' function, which requires identifying 'relevant info structures' like 'general patterns in specific samples' and building relevance from these relevant info structures (like building relevance by combining 'general pattersn of specific samples' and 'error-correcting/minimizing algorithms')
- 'learning/prediction' is a luck-dependant method (which assumes that the 'right info is trivially available to learn' and that 'trivially available info can be used to predict all relevant info'), which can be mostly replaced by 'understanding/derivation/optimization/relevance', although there will still be some prediction involved even when understanding is very good (to base trivial changes on understanding to make valid predictions of the 'trivial remaining unknowns' that are likely to exist in a problem space with very good understanding), until the intent of 'understanding' is solved
- 'optimizing interactions of relevance structures' (like the interaction of 'relevant graphs of a structure' and 'interface analysis applications of a structure' and 'interactions of relevant graphs') is one of the more useful intents to implement

These insights can act like:
- 'organizers/optimizers' of work, like to 'reduce unnecessary/duplicate/irrelevant work' and 'identify important intents'
- 'connections in a graph' that should be applied as a 'constant/requirement/limit graph' (to 'apply as a limit of a possibility space' or 'apply as a component of other graphs', or to 'base changes on, within a relevant degree/area/structure')
