# Mind Control

It's useful to identify variables of mind control/change/other relevant functions.


## Variables

The following are examples of variables related to mind control:
- having a higher degree of variation than the mind, like operating from an outer layer of variation applied to the mind
- understanding the mind to a useful degree (like identifying what the mind will do, what it frequently does, what its defaults are, what its requirements are, where the mind is distributed like in memory, how often the mind is updated, etc)

The following are examples of variables related to mind change:
- level/degree/structure of variation that the mind can support (how strong is the mind, how much variation can it handle, how frequently does it grow or update neurons/memory/weights, what functions does it apply regularly, what is its limit on its potential functionality, what is the rate of generation of relevant variation and integration of relevant variation, etc)


## Functions

Given the variables above, the following example functions are possible:
- creating 'logic loops' that trap a mind in a 'cycle of thought processes' (or otherwise cause 'control structures' to be generated/applied, like barriers/limits/important nodes/requirements)
- creating 'predictability' as in 'probable paths' in a mind that the mind is likelier to use
- creating 'new networks (and incentivized/weighted connections to new networks)' in a mind
