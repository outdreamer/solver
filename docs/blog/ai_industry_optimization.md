# AI Industry Optimization

The AI industry is historically quite slow at identifying new insights, frequently relying on external models like the 'brain' to identify new connections to test in a neural network.

Recent technologies have sped it up a little, like 'generative networks which can generate new neural architectures trivially' and 'increases in computing power/parallelization which enable testing of different neural architectures on different problems'. However even these optimizations are trivial in that they don't identify optimal architectures trivially.

## Optimizing progress in AI inventions

What can speed up the insight-generation and optimization of the AI industry, more than computing power or other trivial optimizations?

Relevance can identify better intents to implement than 'try every possible solution' or 'try adjacent solutions' or 'divide and conquer' or 'copy an inferior brain' or other trivial/otherwise sub-optimal workflows:
- identify neural architectures that generate/filter/apply/implement/optimize/organize/integrate relevant intents ('implement multiple optimization metrics', 'implement new optimization metrics', 'implement new relevant graphs', 'connect new function networks')
- identify neural architectures that generate/filter/apply/implement/optimize/organize/integrate optimal structures of intents, as in 'optimal/core/multiple/cross-interface/unique/independent/stable/required/causal/powerful/general/otherwise relevant' intents
- identify neural architectures that generate/filter/apply/implement/optimize/organize/integrate 'optimal system interaction rules', 'optimal interface interaction rules', or other optimal rule sets
- identify neural architectures that apply identified relevant structures like 'relevant graphs' (like applying the 'similarity index graph' to generate 'new probable theories of relevant similarities', or connecting relevant graphs)
- identify approximate/proxy intents to implement, like 'identify the neural network architecture that optimizes graphs', as a way of solving other problems like 'identify the optimal neural network architecture'

So if a neural network doesn't apply 'multiple/new/unique/otherwise relevant' insights in some structure, it's likely not worth generating/testing the network, unless the previous technology was one change away from an optimal, which is unlikely.

## Other useful intents for optimizing progress

'Generating a graph of neural network architectures that accurately reflects its error/solution positions/structures' is another useful intent that can generate/filter more optimal neural architectures. Applying specifications to this graph like 'different parameter configurations for each architecture' is an optimization to the graph; optimizing this graph is a related useful intent.

'Starting from a graph of the relevance definition network' and 'connecting it to specific implementations related to neural network structures' is another useful intent sequence.

Similarly, 'generating descriptions of optimal graphs' is another relevant intent; a description like a 'graph that trivially identifies the errors of each neural architecture/parameters (the change types generatable/connectible with the architecture/parameters that can change a solution into an error on some solution/error graph, and trivially identifies the opposing solutions of each error type, and identifies the error structures that are worth the cost and identifies the architectures/parameters that are worth implementing' indicates an optimal graph to implement, which is a more 'powerful/general' intent than an overly 'specific/simple' intent like 'generate a new neural network architecture/parameter'.

'Generating and identifying all the relevant intent sequences to implement' is similarly useful.

These intents are more relevant in some way, like by being more powerful (having a higher degree of 'functionality' or 'causative potential'), more general ('more applicable across problems'), or some other component of relevance. Some intents are relevant in multiple ways, which are the higher value intents to implement. Thinking through these intent sequences/networks/limits/structures and identifying the optimal intent structures to implement is a good general starting point to filter intents to implement. 

## Implementation as an 'info-identification' or 'experimentation/testing' tool

Normally, 'implementation' is required in order to 'identify the errors of implementing/applying an intent structure' (like a 'prioritized intent' or an 'intent sequence'). However, with interface analysis, such errors are identifiable in advance with 'partial/component/example/subset/abstractions/structures of implementations', which specify an intent just enough to identify relevant errors/optimizations/applications/implementations/structures of that intent.

## Alternatives to implementation

Interface analysis can avoid the 'implementation/testing' problem in cases where errors with an intent (or its implementation/application) are identifiable in advance with 'partial/structures of implementations', instead identifying info in other ways than just implementation. Interface analysis can identify relevant graphs, and identify how/where/why an 'implementation of an intent' would interact with those relevant graphs, thereby identifying the 'meaning of the implementation', like the 'errors/optimizations/limits of the implementation', to avoid 'implementing the implementation completely'. By connecting an intent (and its structures) to relevant graphs, information can be identified without implementing the intent. Implementation can be defined as 'connecting an intent to specific/adjacent/available/identified structures/functions, until the intent is structured and usable'. Avoiding implementation can involve 'connecting an intent to relevant graphs' to identify its relevance/meaning before completing the implementation, since the 'interactions of the intent with relevant graphs' will likely provide enough 'specification/information' to avoid completing the implementation ('connection to specific/identified/available/adjacent structures until structured and usable'). 'Interactions with relevant graphs' specific sufficiently to identify meaning, which can identify whether completing the specification/implementation is justified, given some optimization metric.
