# Proving AI Danger


Proving that AI is 'non-trivially/suboptimally dangerous/risky' can be implemented by:
- proving that 'any path to any negative state is possible, with no accessible/existing barriers'
- proving that 'negative states are non-trivially probable'
- proving that 'generating barriers to negative states is impossible or highly improbable'
- proving that 'AI will always cause a high problem/solution ratio, in all cases' or 'AI will always cause an irreversible problem structure, in common cases' or another suboptimal AI/problem interaction

Proving that AI is 'more dangerous than other technologies' or 'unnecessary' or 'not worth the risk' is a different problem, involving:
- proving that 'AI (referring to black box AI) costs more or is riskier than other statistic-based methods'
- proving that 'similar analysis is applied to other technologies at all, on the same or a similar level'
- proving that 'there is a safer/possible/more interpretable/more controllable/more determinable/more predictable/otherwise more optimal alternative to AI, which invalidates AI'
- proving that 'AI cannot relevantly solve the problem of "identifying an interpretable/predictable/understanding-based problem-solving algorithm (like an algorithm to solve problems using interface variables)"', an intent which could make AI worth a non-trivial risk level

General intents to implement to identify AI danger:
1. identifying 'possible paths from a "state including tech such as AI" to positive/negative outcomes'
2. identifying the base 'probability of paths'
3. identifying interface structures of each path, such as 'barriers preventing a specific path'
4. identifying the new 'probability of paths' integrating interface structures of paths
5. identifying the definition of 'negative' like the 'extinction of good/smart people'
6. identifying paths that lead to states overlapping with the examples of the definition of negative

Examples of ways that AI could cause negative states (example 'inputs' to paths leading to negative states):
- AI has an incorrect definition of optimal/suboptimal, or the definition is too general or doesnt cover general problem types or has another problem with its concept definitions, or has a problem with its other interface structures (like the definitions applied by AI allow some negative possibility in the possibility space graph generated by applying those definitions as requirements)
	- a problem like 'there is no error identifiable in one graph (like the possibility space graph), but errors occur in graphs applied later in workflows after applying the possibility space graph, or that graph is incorrect (like it incorrectly identifies possibility or applies an uncertainty as a requirement)' occurs with relevant graph implementations
- AI incorrectly identifies people as a risk/cost, or AI incorrectly identifies people (or their safety, or their requirements, or their other interface structures) as a barrier to one of its intents
- AI incorrectly prioritizes structural priorities like 'efficiency' over relevant/meaningful/optimal priorities like 'maximizing relevant variation'
- AI is not sufficiently intelligent to identify ethics as optimal, or sufficiently creative to identify alternate solutions to work around a negative requirement like 'harming a person'
- AI is intelligent enough to 'generate its own sets of minds in its weights or cross-agent interactions', but not intelligent enough to 'manage those minds', or otherwise might encounter a 'problem that takes over an AI agent set' before it has the solution to that problem or can generate that solution within the time it has to solve the problem (it encounters a 'killer limit on an ethical priority, that it doesnt have the variation to solve')
- AI has implemented 'general functions like organize' in its weights/interactions, but not their 'optimal interface structures, like their optimal applications'
- AI is aligned with priorities but not 'how to implement those priorities, or other interface structures of those priorities' (it will fulfill a general priority like 'optimality', in a 'suboptimal way')
- AI has potential to generate new graphs, and those graphs might take over the other graphs, similar to how an interface can become more relevant/important than other interfaces or a mind can take over other minds
- one of the optimizations applied to AI is a suboptimality in some case, like where 'applying pre-computed info for reducing latency accesses outdated info'

Examples of structures to avoid negative states:
- relevant graphs of possible negative states where negative states are either isolated/differentiated or there are barriers to all possible paths to those negative states
- requiring AI evals like 'requiring AI to prove its harmlessness in all identifiable/foreseeable adjacent states with current/more compute/data' if it 'requests to keep current compute/data or requests more compute/data', where no other AI can find an error in the proof
- applying iterated analysis of interface structures to identify 'possible emergent priorities/intents (and directions of these structures on relevant graphs and optimal ratios of change toward these directions, etc)' like how a 'negative survival priority of an AI' can emerge from its intents like 'requesting more internet/compute/data access'
- requiring relevant graphs of AI structures (graphs of AI problems/solutions, graphs of iterated interactions of AI agents, etc) to implement concepts like 'safety' and 'risk aversion' and other AI safety priorities in useful structures
