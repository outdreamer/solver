# Problem-solving questions

The value of a question or query in problem-solving is potentially equivalent to the solution, if the query makes it sufficiently trivial to solve the problem.


## Examples of queries to 'find the connection between a set of math structures'

Examples of normal (low-info, low-relevance/meaning, low optimality) queries to solve a problem like 'find the connection between a set of math structures':
- apply trivial changes of each structure until they're equivalent
- apply a common trick that works in other problems until it's clear whether progress has been made or if progress can't be made further

Examples of valuable queries to solve a problem like 'find the connection between a set of math structures':
- apply a 'component of the definition of relevance' (which hasn't been adjacently connected to these math structures before), formatted to the 'interim/connecting field of math relevant to these math structures' (the component of relevance formatted to the field of math, like what 'uniqueness' or 'independence' or 'optimization' means in that field of math, specifically the field of math where both the math structures are defined or a connecting field of math in between the fields of each math structure)
- identify other math structures which are 'similarly independent/complex/variable' as the 'problem structures to be connected', until a connection composed of similarly independent/complex/variable structures can be identified between the two math structures to connect
- identify a graph where the math structures are 'not in maximally different positions' or 'not unconnectible by directed edges' or otherwise 'trivial/relevant to connect'


## Examples of queries to 'invent new math'

Examples of normal (low-info, low-relevance/meaning, low optimality) queries to solve a problem like 'invent new math':
- apply many iterations of identified variables until a specific simple structural change like 'an increase in dimension' is implemented

Examples of valuable queries to solve a problem like 'invent new math':
- apply maximally different/otherwise independent structures until a structure that is 'different in multiple/powerful/otherwise relevant interfaces' (like the new structure 'requires re-organizing relevant graphs' and 'identifies a new interface or relevant graph' and 'changes the optimality of optimization metrics') is identified, then identify simplifying/otherwise relevant connections to existing structures to identify the position/structure of the newly identified structure in relevant graphs, to identify its uniqueness/newness/other forms of relevance


## Comparing high and low-info queries

It's possible to see how the valuable queries, by applying more information and more implementations of relevance, enable identifying more optimal structures.

Advantages of high-info queries:
- high-info queries do much of the work of solving a problem, by referencing structures already identified to solve problems in general like 'relevant graphs' and the 'definition of relevance' and 'general optimization metrics'
- high-info queries apply highly computable structures, like 'relevant graphs' and 'maximally different variables' and the 'definition of relevance' and 'general optimization metrics'
- high-info queries are extremely likely to solve problems trivially/otherwise relevantly

Disadvantages of high info queries:
- high-info queries apply more structure, which includes 'more assumptions', than low-info queries
- high-info queries require already identifying relevant structures, like 'relevant graphs' and 'maximally different variables' and the 'definition of relevance' and 'general optimization metrics'
- the complexity of the interactions of relevant structures like 'relevant graphs' can increase the complexity of problem-solving in some cases (like where 'iterations of interface structures of relevant graphs' have been applied, but a 'process to connect/similarize/standardize/simplify/systematize/optimize these new iteration interactions' hasnt been applied yet)

Advantages of low-info queries:
- low-info queries may try a larger possibility space (a set of possible solutions)

Disadvantages of low-info queries:
- low-info queries may never find optimal solutions, depending on computation metrics like 'speed of testing solutions' and 'time budget to run the program'
