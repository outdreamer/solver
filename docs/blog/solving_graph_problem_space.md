# Solving the Graph Problem Space

It's useful to identify remaining 'unsolved (or incompletely/otherwise suboptimally solved) problems' in the graph problem space.


## Examples

Examples of remaining unsolved graph intents include:
- graphing the unsolved problems (like by identifying how graphs of 'trivial combinations of interface variable' and the interactions of these graphs are required to be connected optimally to graphs of 'scaled combinations of interface variables' and the interactions of these graphs, bc 'scaled combinations' are likely required to reflect the complexity of reality) and their probable solution structures
- graphing the problem/solution of how 'optimizations/applications/connections/variants/structures' of graphs overlap, to identify optimal generative functions of these structures of graphs
- graphing the problem/solution of how 'graph structures can be generally/optimally optimized' (how specific a graph should be, which graph layers are optimal, etc)
- graphing 'specifications of known solution structures' (like relevant variants of multi-functional graphs) until the problem of 'connecting solution structures' and 'identifying missing solution structures' and related intents are solved
- graphing missing optimal solutions of a graph (like graphing which solution types a graph is suboptimal for, like how a graph might not 'completely/generally solve a problem') as 'problems to generate a new graph to solve', to then integrate 'graphs of problems to generate a new graph to solve'


## Related Intents

Related intents of 'unsolved graph intents' include:
- graphing these 'remaining unsolved graph intents' is useful for related intents like 'defining the optimal graph of relevant graphs'
- applying optimization structures to these intents, like applying changes in 'directions of multi-functionality' across remaining unsolved graph intents
