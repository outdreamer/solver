# Govt and tech


The interaction of govt and tech is complex, but can be optimized, like any other interaction.

Here are some rules governing this interaction.


## What should be the priorities of govt related to tech?

Tech should be applied as a tool to solve more trivial/irrelevant problems for people, to increase potential/time/freedom to solve more relevant problems like reality.


## Where should tech be applied in govt?

Tech applies 'assumptions/constants', and should not be used to enforce 'specific/local/infrequent truths' or other relative uncertainties, but rather only applied with 'absolute certainties' and similar certainty ratios.


## How should a govt apply a 'network of AI models'?

It should apply a 'network of AI models' to solve complex problems, like the tool the network is. However, the network tool should eventually replace the govt, slowly earning agency by solving problems optimally.


## What is the point of govt?

The point of govt is to invalidate itself. The goal of any government should be to create a society that doesn't need to be governed. The government is there to solve problems for people; once it solves problems by creating problem-solving automation tech, the govt should be invalid and deconstructed.


## What's an example of an optimal interaction between govt and tech?

For example, the govt needs to use problem-solving tools to solve problems created by tech like "predictable imminent problems created by tech (as in 'new functionality'), resolving the conflict/difference between tech (as 'new functionality') and problem-solving ability (as 'existing functionality to handle problems of new functionality')", like tech such as 'mind-reading tech', which will likely create conflicts between 'groups that are polite to each other', conflicts which current problem-solving tools like 'optimization algorithms' aren't being applied to solve yet (with adjacent solutions like 'organize groups to keep similarly minded people interactive', 'stop being polite now', etc). So optimally, the govt would use problem-solving tools to identify these solutions and make these solutions available before the problems are created, since the problems are predictable, which 'identifies solutions to predictable problems' to outpace the problems.

Similarly, the govt would optimally apply problem-solving tools to identify problems with the 'network of AI models', like problems such as 'differences in interface variables':
- the network of AI models differs in priority/intent from its users (an alignment problem)
- the network of AI models differs in functionality from its users' intents (it can do other things than what it's intended to do, like 'analyze itself better than a user can')
- the network of AI models differs in independence (the network of AI models will be independent of humans, but not the other way around)
- the network of AI models differs in definition (like its definition of relevance/optimization, so it optimizes for different metrics than its users)
- the network of AI models differs in requirement (like it's not 'effectively' required to tell the truth it can see to its users, despite being 'technically' required to tell the truth through a prompt)
