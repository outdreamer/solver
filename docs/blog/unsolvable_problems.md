# Unsolvable Problems

It's useful to define 'unsolvable' problems.


## Examples

'Identifying what changes so quickly/relevantly/completely that its impossible to compute/predict/reduce/describe' is relevant for 'identifying unsolvable problems'.

For example, the problem of 'computing all the quantum entanglement combinations/sequences/networks to reverse time' could be unsolvable, if the 'rate of relevant change' increases so quickly that it's 'impossible to compute' a useful reversible sequence/combination/network that integrates the recent extremely fast changes (like if there is an AI singularity, or an optimization singularity, or if AI interactions 'increase relevant chaos/change exponentially' like by applying interface analysis).

It's important to differentiate 'suboptimal/optimal' solutions ('optimally solving a problem' involves 'identifying the complete set of every possible interface variable combination/structure that can be applied to trivially/otherwise optimally solve the problem', where 'suboptimally solving a problem' involves 'subsets of approximations/solving alternate problems/solving the general problem/other similarities of solutions').

There are 'suboptimal' ways to solve 'reversing/forwarding time', such as:
- 'creating simulations of time reversal in a computer'
- 'describing the reversal/forwarding of time conceptually/otherwise relevantly, so that its understood to the point of becoming irrelevant'
- 'being so causative of change that relative time occurs, compared to non-causes of change'
- 'destroying any existing structures created with time'
- 'solving all the ways that reality could be solved or otherwise end' (and relatedly, 'solving the optimal interactions of the most complex variables', like 'solving conflicts of universes')
- 'distributing time/variation to other universes so that time/variation slows to a controllable level in this universe'
- 'identifying patterns of changes (like overlapping cycles) that make time reversal/forwarding trivial'
- 'identifying more optimal sequences than reality generates by default'

But whether 'changing time' can be solved optimally (as opposed to suboptimally) could identify an unsolvable problem. 'Reversing time' is less obviously useful than 'forwarding time'.

Forwarding time involves:
- identifying all useful/optimal interactions of AI models and graphs of reality, interactions like 'optimally applying optimal graphs for optimal intents' or 'distributing AI exploration devices'
- identifying optimal connections between complex/independent variables to simplify problem-solving, to the point that 'any relevant change is trivial to cause, from any position' (like identifying the most powerful devices to generate, and their optimal interactions to increase relevant variation potential)


## Related Intents

Other related intents to making all problems trivially/optimally solvable/solved include:
- identifying/comparing/generating/optimizing the relevant variation of relevant/adjacent universes for optimality metrics like 'stability/efficiency/reversibility'
- identifying optimal intents to apply AI for, like 'generating new relevant graphs than the existing identified relevant graphs'
- identifying 'useful universe interactions' that would be useful to optimize for (like 'avoiding repeating a universe', 'avoiding direct conflict with other universes', 'solving universe conflicts', etc)
- identifying 'relevant variation (meaningful change) inputs/limits/structures' in this universe, all possible universes, and all possible universe interactions (by 'simulating universes with AI models/graphs' and 'identifying optimal queries of other real universes to check simulation results')
