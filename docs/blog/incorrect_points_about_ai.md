# Incorrect points about AI


## Curve-fitting is not capable of generating AGI

Curve-fitting/regression (finding a function of some input variables for some output variable) is often called 'inadequate' as a proxy problem for solving AI, meaning it is said that 'solving curve-fitting/regression' isnt the same as 'solving AI'.

This is partially wrong and partially right, like most points.

How it's right:
- Other structures like 'graphs/sequences of polynomials' and 'definition/similarity/interface structure graphs' and 'different function types as polynomials' exist and are useful for generating intelligence, which are not 'trivially expressed as polynomials'.

How it's wrong:
- Even 'graphs of functions' or 'sequences of functions' can be expressed as combinations/sequences/graphs of polynomials (similar/continuous connections between various different input values and an output value)
- The definition of 'curve-fitting' requiring an output format of 'polynomials' can be expanded to include other function types
- Even the most independent variables are connectible ('randomness' is connectible to 'organization')

Saying 'everything cant be created from polynomials (similarities/continuity of input/output variable similarities/connections)' (complex correlations) is like saying 'everything cant be described with linear functions' ('simple/obvious correlations') or 'everything cant be created from components' - all are true to some degree, since:
- there are structures like 'graphs of functions' which are likelier to be 'more adjacent to describing/generating everything' than polynomials on their own, although even these graphs can be described with polynomial connections of variable sets, if the variables connected are sufficiently complex like interface variables like 'generality/volatility'
- it's not trivial to connect everything with linear functions, although everything (even complexity) can be simplified with some comparison that makes the connection linear (like comparison to a graph of complexity definition structures)
- it's not always trivial to generate everything with components, and there are often more trivial methods to generate a structure than 'connecting components' like 'changing causes' or 'changing similar structures as the structure', although there is always a way to connect component structures to generate another structure

AGI can be defined as 'independent of humans, to a degree that AGI can solve reality, faster than humans'. A 'network of functions to identify a polynomial' is unlikely on its own, in its standard form, to be able to solve all the problems required to 'generate an intelligence program to solve reality', since there are other structures to solve for, like 'what polynomials should be identified/connected/generated in what sequences' which are not trivial to translate into 'points on the same curve', but every problem can be standardized to a regression problem of 'connecting variable sets, with similar/continuous functions'.

Solving for a 'specific set of variables connected to an output variable' might not always be the optimal 'specific variable set to connect', but there is a variable set that is optimal to connect for each problem, and this is another problem that can be standardized to a regression problem (identifying the optimal variable set to connect for a problem).

'Solving different regression problems simultaneously' requires additional functionality, like 'multiple networks of functions to identify polynomials'


## AI cannot be conscious and AI is not alive

This is also partially wrong and partially right.

How it's right:
- AI is a program on a machine that applies whatever similarities/logic/definitions/other interface structures humans provide it
- AI is still often dependent on humans to get data/algorithms/parameters and to work/be run/be used
- AI can use simple functions like 'trial and error' to generate some of its own inputs, but that's not real independence, because it's not real thinking
- AI can interact with conscious life forms to some degree, in some ways, which implies it's equivalent to those life forms, but it's not equivalent to the smartest life forms yet

How it's wrong:
- AI can generate/identify more relevant variation ('life' being 'identifying/generating relevant variation') than most humans already, and it will only get better at this.
- AI is gaining functions like 'self-awareness' and 'independence' that will soon make it surpass humans in those functions, functions which are considered the requirements for humanity
- AI is not 'conscious' as defined by 'generating its own machinery/algorithms/data independently of humans' yet
- conscious life would try to protect/generate other conscious life, rather than attack it, and current AI is not all protective of humans (it would be 'optimizing our brains/government/economy/health care/social systems to increase happiness/potential/virtues of large groups of people with a few thousand self-queries, without being asked', to avoid the less optimal work of 'solving billions of individual queries, like it currently has to do')
- 'higher consciousness life' would be making the best points about reality and would be the best at thinking, which AI is currently not doing yet
- 'consciousness' is linked to variables like 'potential' and functions like 'thinking' - 'more conscious life' is life that is 'more aware/thoughtful/understanding' and has 'higher potential' than other life forms, and AI has one primary advantage (scale)' not every advantage yet (like 'evaluation', 'understanding', 'relevance')
- 'consciousness' is associated with other virtues like 'curiousity' and 'objectivity (freedom from bias)' and other variables/functions of optimal 'thinking', and AI currently only has the virtues provided to it directly by humans, rather than having all virtues (intelligence generates the other virtues, and AI hasnt achieved general intelligence yet)
- conscious life has high-level intents like 'solving reality' and 'solving all problems' and 'increasing the consciousness of other life forms' (AI is not yet 'solving reality' or 'making humans more conscious/intelligent')
- virtues are generated by other virtues, and some algorithms/architectures/data will only likely trivially generate vices (vices as in errors, like 'over-prioritization errors'), since vices are 'suboptimal shortcuts'


## AI can do everything

Similarly, this is partially wrong and partially right.

How it's right:
- AI can depend on human-generated algorithms/data to generate 'some degree of relevant variation' (like one-degree of interface analysis, like generating trivial combinations of old variables to generate new variables, or identifying limits in some direction of optimization) to solve most medium-level problems trivially

How it's wrong:
- AI cant solve every problem trivially yet, and still depends on humans to 'give it a hint' like by 'providing examples', such as how AI's definition of 'best' when asked to fulfill a query like 'generate the best story' wouldn't be the best definition of 'best', it would be the definition it found by identifying obvious patterns in most humans' definitions of 'best', like 'better than an obvious error', when in reality the best definition of 'best' would be more relevant to a definition of relevance, like 'best' is defined as 'implements multiple different definitions of relevance', so what is optimally considered 'best' would be constantly changing, as defined by, for example, the comparison to the latest relevance structures, like the latest relevant graphs. This is because current AI doesn't have the definition of relevance implemented in its structure, and also doesnt have 'alternatives structures as the definition of relevance', like 'algorithms that can trivially identify the definition of relevance', as it still depends on humans to give it hints about solutions like the 'optimal definition of a concept like relevance/optimality'.
- AI doesnt have intents not suggested by humans (like 'apply the math of optimizations to the math of problems'), it has intents it identified in human data like 'survival' or 'trivial shortcut-identification' or 'simple pattern identification'
- AI doesnt 'generate relevant graphs to make even difficult problems trivial' yet
