# Solution/Error graphs

Graphs of solution/error structures can be implicit (where connections are implied to be 'solutions' as 'connections between differences') or explicit (where nodes/connections are explicitly defined as solution/error types or other solution/error structures).


## Default solution/error graphs

Core solution/error graphs include graphs with core structures connecting solutions/errors, like 'error node layers surrounding solution nodes', to represent realistic patterns of solutions/errors in complex systems, like how 'trivial variants of a solution' are often errors.

Stochastically generated solution/error graphs are useful for identifying algorithms to differentiate solutions/errors with minimal pattern information acting as hints.

Solution/error graphs where solutions are explicitly defined as 'maximally different (like maximally distant) from errors' to reflect variables of realistic systems like 'complexity of problem-solving' are useful to identify algorithms to identify complex system interaction rules and algorithms to solve realistically complex problems.


## Relevant graphs as solution/error graphs

Relevant graphs by definition connect 'maximal/other relevant differences' in a 'trivial/otherwise relevant way', and are therefore able to solve a high ratio of problems trivially/otherwise relevantly. Relevant graphs are like an interface in this way, in that they connect sufficient differences to be generally capable of solving all problems.

Relevant graphs often optimize for different subsets of optimization metrics, and often connect different difference types, and are relevantly different in other ways, making each relevant graph significantly different from the other relevant graphs, and are capable of generating/identifying the other relevant graphs (like by varying the variables of relevant graphs, like varying the 'subset of optimization metrics optimized for in a relevant graph', or by applying relevant differences like 'relevant opposites/errors' to relevant graphs to identify its limits/definitions).

Given that relevant graphs solve a problem by 'relevantly/trivially connecting/filtering/otherwise solving relevant/maximal differences', they represent solution/error structures by default. A 'connection' on a relevant graph might represent a solution if it connects error metrics with solution metrics, or a node on a relevant graph might represent a solution if it's a graph of optimization structures, or a 'compression' of a relevant graph might represent a solution.

Explicitly applying a relevant graph as a solution/error graph by explicitly applying solution/error structures in the graph (like by 'replacing default node types with solution types' or 'optimizing the graph to connect a higher ratio of solution/error types') usually increases the relevance of the graph, by connecting it directly to relevant structures like errors/solutions, similar to how 'connecting relevant graphs' and 'identifying graphs of relevant graphs' increases their relevance.


## Neural networks with solution/error structures

Solution/error structures can be applied in neural networks like:
- applying common standardized solution polynomials of 'connections between variables sets in realistic systems' as default weights in a neural network
- applying 'vectors from solution/error graphs' as 'vectors in neural networks'
- applying 'relevant graphs (of solutions/errors)' as 'benchmarks' of neural networks (as in 'can the neural network learn the relevant graph trivially/relevantly')
