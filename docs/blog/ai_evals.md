# AI Evals


What are remaining intents to test AI on?

Beyond the queries involving structures like 'relevant graphs' and 'optimizations' in this repo, there are useful general intent structures to apply to generate AI evals.


## Example intent sequence as a set of relevant AI evals

Whether AI models can complete the following intents optimally is useful to identify:
- identify general relevant intents (like 'solve reality' or 'complete the math interface' or 'identify relevant interface structures, like relevant graphs')
- identify relevant graphs like 'optimized/organized graphs' of these intents
- identify relevant 'intent networks' to implement/reach these general relevant intents
- identify relevant interface structures (like limits/optimizations/errors) of the intent networks, to generate relevant graph structures like 'graph layers (like associated optimization metrics with intents)'
- identify 'specific identified functions' to identify/generate connections to 'relevant graphs of the intent networks of general intents' or to 'correct errors in the intent networks'


## Current intent optimization/implementation

Currently AI models implement specific local intents (like 'formatting a file' or 'summarizing a dataset').

Current AI models including LLMs often solve these problems with an 'over-simplified, structural definition of relevance'.

This is similar to the problem of 'solving math' by 'identifying core functions (like multiplication) and applying them iteratively', where it's more useful to identify concepts to understand math in general first (concepts like 'variability' and 'stability' and 'uncertainty'). 

This involves approaching the problem from a different starting position on the interface network.
