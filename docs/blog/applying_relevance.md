# Applying relevance

Applying relevance involves identifying prioritized relevance structures, as in 'what structure is more relevant than other structures, in what cases'.

This involves ranking relevance structures by priority and identifying relevant 'combinations/sequences/networks' of priorities of relevance structures, like 'first identify constants, then variables, then variables of constants'.

For example, it's useful to differentiate deceptively similar structures, like identifying when a decision is 'really a decision' or is actually a very different structure like 'required', which makes it irrelevant whether it's a decision or not, because what is relevant is that it's required. The 'requirement' structure takes priority over the 'decision' structure.

Then it's useful to identify structures that could 'invalidate the requirement' or 'change the priority/interface structures of the requirement' or otherwise 'be relevant to the requirement':
- identify whether there is an adjacent change that could make the requirement not required
- identify whether the requirement is really required, or just falsely seems to be and there are actually alternatives
- identify whether the requirement should be required
- identify whether the requirement is required on every relevant graph or just a subset of relevant graphs, which would make it a 'more required' requirement if it was 'required on more relevant graphs'
- identify whether the 'intent fulfilled by the requirement' or the 'case causing the intent to be relevant' or 'other inputs of the requirement' are required
- identify variables of the requirement, like whether its 'generally or specifically' or 'explicitly or implicitly' required

These structures could 'change the relevance of' the requirement, so they are relevant to the requirement.
