# Justice


Relevant questions to identify property rights (as in 'contribution/value/credit/justice'):
- who made a unique contribution (like a 'contribution that no one else could contribute'), which asks the question 'who is depended on the most, or who is the most necessary/required'
- who is capable of doing the same work as trivially/relevantly or more trivially/relevantly
- who did the actual required work in reality (in opposition to merely 'being capable of doing that work')
- who made a contribution that differs from what is currently automated or trivially/relevantly automatable (like 'who is automating automation')
- who optimally did the work (who can do the work in a valuable way, like quickly, or first, or in a way that solves other problems as well)
- who can do more valuable work than the work, and who can do the most valuable work
- who deserves protection/rights (like bc they already did valuable work or can do valuable work)
- who can iterate that value/contribution (who can do other valuable work, like inventing another valuable invention, as opposed to only having one contribution)
- who can optimally use the work (as in 'who can use it for the most valuable intents')
- who can automate/optimize the work, bc they understand it so optimally
- who can integrate the work optimally into existing valuable systems (who can format the work into a set of relevant graphs optimally)
- who has the fewest requirements (who needs the least property, meaning theyre an efficient value contributor with few input requirements, meaning property has the highest 'return on investment' when it's given to that contributor)
- who is the best general work optimizer (who identifies the most valuable intents to work on, allocating their attention optimally)
- who does the most 'general/specific/other interface variable' work the best (who identifies the general intent/relevance and who implements the specific variant of the work the best)
- who depends on luck the least (who needs the least luck to succeed at contributing value, like being 'lucky enough to have learned some info, like a simple explanation, that helps them complete some other task')
- who is the best at generally useful functions like 'learning/deriving/inventing/optimizing/thinking' that enable 'general/specific potential to do valuable work'
- who has the most optimal potential to be good at contributing valuable work (as in 'it will take minimal change to enable them to do valuable work')
- who can define valuable work optimally
- who can do valuable work, and always chooses to do it, so they effectively can do valuable work (who avoids crimes/scams and instead solves useful problems)
- who depends on lies the least (whose claims are the most accurate/valid/true/relevant/verifiable)
- who can use existing resources (like existing machines/algorithms) to do the most valuable work (as opposed to needing future tools to have potential to do valuable work)
- who can do absolutely useful work (as opposed to relatively/comparatively useful work)
- who causes the most valuable work to be completed by other people
- who enjoys doing valuable work the most, meaning they are the likeliest to actually do that valuable work
- who has natural property/resources/value (intelligence) and who has the right to those resources by nature (who should be made intelligent)
- what 'groups/other social structures' have contributed valuable work (where the group is 'required to work as a group' to contribute)
- what 'definition of property rights' would filter out useful/required intents like 'space exploration' (defining 'justice' too specifically might preclude intents like 'space exploration', which is non-trivial to identify)

These questions can identify 'how to allocate property currently' and "how to change its allocation to an optimal allocation, in a sequence to avoid cases like 'mass theft/injustice'".

'Algorithms to allocate property optimally' can solve the problems of 'markets' and 'justice', invalidating a subset of government problems.

This can also solve the problem of 'identifying valuable intents to work on' by solving 'filtering optimal intents', intents like 'making some group of people smarter'.

Generally, this set of questions will 'reward smart/good people', and will 'maximize their group size ratio' and otherwise 'maximize the potential/power of that group' to generally increase justice/value/optimality/meaning.
