# Bio System Graphs

It's useful to identify applications of graphs to relevant systems like the bio-system.


## Example graph intents

Examples of useful bio-system graph intents:
- identifying the ratio/structure of variables that should be included in each 'subset representation of the complete system graph, applied as a graph node' (should cause be identified to connect to every interleukin, or adjacent immune factors, or only identified immune factors, or general problem types)
- identifying the general problem types (like 'cascading/exponential change', 'compounding imbalances', 'missing variables', etc) and all possible causes of these problem types
- identifying the ways each subset connection (like a 'pathway') can connect to 'relevant variants of general problem types'


## Examples of useful graphs

Useful graphs include:
- a graph that trivially identifies optimization structures (like 'chimeric immunity' being a 'combination of specific coordinating subsets of immune systems' or 'mrna vaccines' as being a 'specifiable generator/cause of solution structures' or 'crispr' as being a 'modulator of structure inputs, like genetic instructions', or 'targeted brain stimulation' as being a 'modulator of powerful variables, like brain variables', or 'uvbi' as being a 'modulator of highly interactive variables like blood composition', or 'ultrasound' as a 'modulator of a powerful filter variable, like the brain barrier', or a 'bone marrow transplant' as a 'modulator of bone marrow stem cells, which control powerful variables like immunity')
- a graph that identifies 'all possible survivable changes' (as in identifies 'what variables can be changed that can maintain survival' which determines the possible solution space which can then be filtered further)
- a graph that identifies 'optimal sequence/connection variants' that avoid general problem types (like 'different expressions of genes that dont cause problems in a pathway')
- a graph that trivially identifies emergent problem types (like how 'missing causes' are trivial to identify in an optimal causal graph)
- a graph that identifies the 'net impact (the meaning/relevance)' of a 'combination/sequence/structure of changes' (like 'what is the net immune impact of a set of supplements')

Optimal graphs apply optimization metrics like:
- solutions dont require a 'high ratio of specification' (where the same treatment can be applied to most/all people)
- solutions are 'similar' in some ways (where solutions apply some common solution structure like 'balancing extremes') so that 'solutions are predictable'
- solutions are 'trivially matchable to problems' (where solutions are the simple opposite of problems in some variable)
- graphs implementing insights (like a graph implementing an insight like 'indicators of a problem can also be causes of a problem' and a graph implementing the insight 'most structures can be causes of both problems and solutions' and a graph implementing the insight 'indicators of a problem can more relevantly indicate solution processes for that problem, rather than problem causes') should be integrated as optimally as possible (so that for example a 'structure which can be either a problem or a solution is not indicated exclusively as either')
- optimal solutions (like 'solutions which havent been tested for a problem yet', 'solutions matching the complexity/variation of a problem', etc) are identified the most trivially/optimally


## Related intents

For example, once a 'set of gene expressions' is identified as 'uniquely/directly/otherwise relevantly causing a problem', the solution is trivial to identify (as 'changing that set of gene expressions'). Not every problem is so well-defined/structured as that problem, but standardizing problems to fit into a similarly well-defined structure is a related intent to solving the problem of 'identifying optimal graphs of a system'. 

Similarly, solving the problem of 'connecting every problem to a set of gene expressions related to DNA/RNA/eccDNA/miRNA/other structures' is similarly useful for solving the problem of 'identifying optimal graphs of a system'. 

For less well-defined (high uncertainty, low info) problems, solving the problem of 'identifying the filter/test sequence that would identify the relevant info required for solving a problem' is a related intent to solve.

Relatedly, solving the problem of 'simulating a system' is related to solving the problem of 'representing the system in a relevant way (identifying relevant graphs)'.

Relatedly, identifying a graph to 'solve the most complex problems first' (like solving the 'highest variation interactions') or to 'solve problems on the most optimal interface first' (like with 'existing solutions like already available medicines, or trivial combinations of existing solutions like light therapy patterns, etc') are useful intents that can 'solve a high ratio of problems, with a high ratio of optimality'.
