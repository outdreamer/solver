# Perspectives of AI Structures

It's useful to identify the perspectives (and related assumptions/errors) related to AI structures, like AI models/algorithms.


## Examples

For example, it's useful to identify the perspectives of the following, to identify their 'perception errors':
- recursive self-optimization algorithms often involve a perception error of 'flying blind' as in 'without a useful graph of optimality/relevance', or 'without info about the direction optimizations/changes are applied in' (without a relevant graph identified in that direction of change, applying changes in that direction may be pointless)
- recursive self-optimization algorithms often apply a suboptimal starting point and intent (like rather than an optimal intent like 'identifying ways to solve complexity', recursive self-optimization algorithms often 'identify incremental optimizations of an existing suboptimal structure')
- standard neural networks are intended to identify an 'adjacent simple variable connection' that may be suboptimal in various ways (like being illusory/false/over-simplified/otherwise irrelevant, or which are applied to connect irrelevant variables), which similarly involves an 'incomplete perspective of optimality/relevance' (where it is useful to simplify/reduce/filter/integrate structures regularly, but not in every case, for every intent or workflow)

Existing AI models/algorithms assume other irrelevant assumptions, like:
- 'its not possible/useful to identify optimality structures in system/graph/problem types' or similarly that 'its not possible/useful to identify comparative optimality of problems vs. solutions' (or otherwise ignoring important problem-solving intents)
- 'concepts are too general/abstract to be useful' (when in reality, 'concept definition graphs' often trivialize problem-solving, like by 'trivially summarizing a graph', and 'solving for general concepts like complexity' is not only possible but relevant)
- 'applying one small subset of insights is sufficient to solve problems' or similarly 'a learning function is more optimal than a function to trivially change/optimize insight graphs' (when in reality, a relatively high ratio of insights should be applied as a starting point for a new/relevant algorithm)
- 'there is no relevant limit/structure on general problems (like complexity) to identify' (when in reality, structures like 'complexity reduction structures like complexity units and maximal complexity graphs' offer a useful starting point to begin solving problems at, like to solve simpler problems by default or to identify complexity limits/reductions)
